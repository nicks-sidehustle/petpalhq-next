A remote training collar lets an owner send a cue to a dog at a distance: a tone, a vibration and, on most units, static stimulation. This guide is for everyday owners working on recall and attention in a backyard or a park, not for hunting or field work. It ranks five remote trainers on how much they let you do with tone and vibration alone, because that is where the veterinary behavior guidance points.

Start with what the evidence says. AVSAB's position statement says: "There is no evidence that aversive training is necessary for dog training or behavior modification." A 2020 controlled study in Frontiers in Veterinary Science reached a similar conclusion for remote collars specifically: "These findings refute the suggestion that training with an E-collar is either more efficient or results in less disobedience, even in the hands of experienced trainers." Reward-based training comes first, and nothing on this page replaces it.

These picks are editorial synthesis. PetPalHQ has not trained a dog with any of them. Each was chosen by reading maker spec pages and Amazon listings against AVSAB, ASPCA and BC SPCA guidance, and every spec on this page comes from those sources.

## Tone and Vibration First

The ASPCA says: "The ASPCA is opposed to any training equipment that causes a pet to experience physical discomfort or undue anxiety." That is why the top pick has no static stimulation at all, and why the second pick earns its place with a maker-published vibration-only setup. Used as a cue, a tone or a vibration works like a clicker the dog can hear or feel across a yard: pair it with a treat or a game until the dog turns toward you when it fires.

## If a Trainer Recommends Static Stimulation

AVSAB's position statement lists electronic shock collars among the training tools that involve pain, which it says an appropriate trainer should avoid, and the BC SPCA does not support shock collars for dogs. If a professional trainer still recommends static stimulation for your dog, ask why tone and vibration are not enough, use the lowest level the dog notices, and choose a unit with fine steps and a level lock. Those are the two things this guide's control factor measures.

## Remote Trainer or GPS Collar

None of these picks tracks location. If the dog ranges out of sight in the field, or you need to find it, the [GPS dog training e-collar guide](/guides/best-gps-dog-training-e-collar-systems-2026) covers GPS units, and the [dog GPS trackers guide](/guides/best-dog-gps-trackers-2026) covers tracking alone. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is there a remote dog training collar with no shock?**
A: Yes. The Educator PG-300 Pager Only sends a tone or a vibration, with 100 levels of vibration according to E-Collar Technologies, and has no static stimulation. The Dogtra 200iQ does have stim built in, but Dogtra says to remove the contacts for vibration-only training.

**Q: Do vets and behavior groups support e-collars?**
A: The groups cited here do not. AVSAB lists electronic shock collars among tools that involve pain, the BC SPCA does not support shock collars for dogs, and the ASPCA opposes training equipment that causes physical discomfort or undue anxiety. That is why this guide ranks units on what they can do without stim.

**Q: Will a remote collar train recall faster than treats?**
A: The 2020 study in Frontiers in Veterinary Science found that training with an e-collar was not more efficient and did not result in less disobedience than reward-focused training, even with experienced trainers. A remote collar does not shortcut the work of building a recall.

**Q: Which remote collar suits a small dog?**
A: E-Collar Technologies rates the ET-300 Mini for dogs 5 lbs and up, the lowest published minimum among these picks. The FieldTrainer 425X starts at 8 pounds, and both Dogtra units at 10 lbs+. The specs this guide drew on do not give a dog-size rating for the PG-300, so check the receiver against your dog's neck.

**Q: Can I use a remote training collar in water?**
A: SportDOG says the FieldTrainer 425X remote and collar are both waterproof and submersible to 25 feet, and Dogtra describes the 280X as fully waterproof and drop-proof. Confirm current price and availability before buying.
