A 55 to 75 gallon tank is where many freshwater keepers end up once a small tank feels cramped. The extra water is a real advantage: Tetra's Learning Center says the larger the aquarium you own, the easier it is to keep the water balanced and healthy. But a tank this size is also heavy furniture, and the box it ships in often holds only the glass. This guide compares four kits and tanks from 55 to 72 gallons on what ships with them, how much water they hold, and how much of each one a buyer can confirm before ordering.

Few new tanks in this size class turned up in our Amazon searches. One is a complete Tetra 55-gallon kit with a filter, heater and lights. The other three are bare tanks: a Tetra 55-gallon and two ultra clear rimless tanks at 55 and 72 gallons. A 66-gallon tank with a built-in overflow was left out because an overflow drains to a sump, and its listing title gives no sign it is meant as a freshwater display.

These picks are editorial synthesis. PetPalHQ does not run a testing lab, and no tank here was set up by us. Each was chosen by reading its Amazon listing, and Tetra's own kit page where one exists, against guidance from the Merck Veterinary Manual, Tetra and SaltwaterAquarium.com. Confirm current price and availability before buying.

## Plan the Stand Before the Tank

The Merck Veterinary Manual puts the weight problem plainly: "Because 1 gallon (3.8 L) of water weighs 8.3 lb (3.8 kg), even a small aquarium can be extremely heavy relative to its size." SaltwaterAquarium.com's standard tank table estimates a 55-gallon at 625 lbs filled and a 75-gallon at 850 lbs, and it adds that "The weights are an estimate because the amount of rock, thickness of glass and type of water all effect the weight calculations." None of the picks here includes a stand. Merck's rule for what goes underneath is short: the floor and the stand must be strong, level and stable. Our [aquarium stands guide](/guides/best-aquarium-stands-cabinets-2026) ranks stands by rated capacity for tanks up to 75 gallons.

## Kit or Bare Tank?

Only the Tetra kit ships with the gear to run it. Tetra's page for the kit lists a Whisper PF 60 filter, a 200-watt heater, a thermometer and two hinged LED hoods. The three bare tanks need all of that bought separately, and the heater is the item to size carefully: Tetra's advice is to "Be sure to purchase a heater that is properly rated wattage-wise for your tank." Our [aquarium heater guide](/guides/best-aquarium-heaters-2026), [canister filter guide](/guides/best-aquarium-canister-filters-2026) and [planted aquarium light guide](/guides/best-planted-aquarium-lights-2026) cover that gear; check each product's rated tank size against your tank before buying.

## Footprint Matters as Much as Gallons

Two tanks with the same volume can hold different numbers of fish. The Merck Veterinary Manual's freshwater guideline ties stocking to surface area, 1 inch of fish for every 12 square inches, so a wide, deep tank gives more room to stock than a long, narrow one. Only the Tetra kit's dimensions were confirmed for this guide, so read the Product Details on any of the bare tanks before you plan a stocking list.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How much does a filled 55 gallon aquarium weigh?**
A: SaltwaterAquarium.com's table estimates a standard 55-gallon at 625 lbs filled, and a 75-gallon at 850 lbs. The site says those figures are estimates that change with rock, glass thickness and the type of water. Buy a stand rated well above the filled weight and put it on a strong, level floor.

**Q: How many fish can a 55 gallon tank hold?**
A: It depends on the water surface, not just the gallons. Merck's freshwater guideline is 1 inch of fish for every 12 square inches of surface area. Applied to the outside measurements Tetra gives for its kit's tank, 48 by 13 inches, that is roughly 52 inches of fish, and the true water surface is slightly smaller than the outside measurements. Treat it as a ceiling to stock toward slowly, not a target.

**Q: Is a 55 gallon tank good for a beginner?**
A: The size itself helps. Tetra's Learning Center says the larger the aquarium you own, the easier it is to keep the water balanced and healthy. The harder parts for a beginner are the weight, the stand and buying the right gear, which is why the Tetra kit, with its filter, heater and hoods listed on Tetra's page, ranks first here. For a smaller first tank, the starter checklist on this site walks through a 20-gallon setup.

**Q: Do rimless tanks need a lid?**
A: Neither rimless listing title mentions a lid, so plan as if none is included. The titles describe the tank only. Check the listing for what ships, and if you want a cover, size it to the tank's exact outside dimensions from the Product Details.

**Q: Why is there no 75 gallon tank on this list?**
A: The 75-gallon listings that turned up in this guide's Amazon research were a center-overflow tank built around a sump and a sump conversion kit, not freshwater display tanks. The 72-gallon rimless tank is the largest display tank here that passed the October 3, 2026 check.
