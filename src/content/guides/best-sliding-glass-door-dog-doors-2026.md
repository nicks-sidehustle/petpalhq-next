A sliding glass door looks like the hardest door in the house to add a dog door to, because nobody wants to cut into the glass. A patio panel insert avoids cutting anything. It is a tall, narrow panel with a pet door built into it that stands in the slider's existing track. AKC Reunite notes that "Pet doors can be installed in walls rather than doors, and some are designed to be installed in sliding doors." This guide covers only that sliding-door type: manual, non-electronic panels that need no cutting.

Choosing one comes down to three measurements and one question. First, the door frame, because each panel adjusts across a range of only a few inches, and a 96-inch door needs a tall version. Second, the dog, because the flap opening and weight rating have to clear your dog. Third, the rise, the height of the step at the bottom of the opening. The question is how you will close the opening, because a flap alone does not keep other animals out.

These picks are editorial synthesis. PetSafe Support's sizing guidance and the American Kennel Club's dog-door advice set the standard, and each panel's Amazon listing specifications were read against it. PetPalHQ does not run a pet-door testing lab, and nothing here was installed for this guide. Where a listing figure is not among the verified facts this guide drew on, it is recorded as missing rather than estimated.

## Measure the Door Before the Dog

Start with the door frame, because a panel that does not fit it cannot go in at all. Every panel here with a published height range adjusts across a narrow band. The PetSafe standard-height panels cover 75 7/8 to 80 11/16 inches, the Ideal Pet 10-1/2 x 15 panel covers 77-5/8 to 80-3/8 inches, and PetSafe Support gives its 96-inch version a range of 91 7/16 to 96 inches. Ideal Pet's listing says to measure flush at the top track to the inside bottom track of the frame.

Two more checks decide whether the panel sits properly. The PetSafe tall panel's listing says the track must be at least 1 inch deep and that the panel extends 1 3/8 inches from the frame once the closing panel is installed. Both Ideal Pet panels are for aluminum sliders. If your slider is vinyl or wood, cross them off before comparing anything else.

## Then Size the Flap for the Dog

The American Kennel Club puts the sizing risk plainly: "A dog door that is too small could be frightening or even dangerous for a dog if they were to get stuck going in or out of the dog door." That is why this guide weights dog size highest. Both PetSafe Sliding Glass Pet Door Large panels (the standard-height and the 96-inch) are rated for pets up to 100 lb; the Extreme Weather panel's rating is not among the verified facts. Both Ideal Pet panels are rated for pets up to 90 pounds. The Ideal Pet 10-1/2 x 15 listing also gives a maximum pet width of 10.5 inches, the easiest check to run with a tape measure.

Rise is the figure most listings skip. PetSafe Support defines it as "the height of the piece your dog will have to step over when going through the pet door from the ground to the bottom of the pet door opening." Among the five picks, only the Ideal Pet 10-1/2 x 15 panel publishes it in the facts this guide drew on, at 8 inches. For a short-legged or stiff older dog, find the rise on the listing before ordering.

## Closing the Opening at Night

A pet door is an opening, and AKC Reunite says "It is not unusual for other people’s pets, wildlife, and even people to enter homes through pet doors." The picks handle that differently. Both PetSafe Large panels come with a closing panel, the Extreme Weather panel comes with a security bar, and the Ideal Pet Modular panel has an animal lock-out slide. No closing part is among the verified facts for the Ideal Pet 10-1/2 x 15 panel, so plan your own way to block that opening.

None of these panels decides who comes through. If raccoons, a neighbor's dog or a second pet that should stay in are the problem, a manual flap is the wrong tool. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I put a dog door in a sliding glass door without cutting the glass?**
A: Yes. Every pick here is a patio panel insert that stands in the existing track. The PetSafe Large panel's listing describes a do-it-yourself install without cutting into walls or doors, and the tall panel's listing says it needs no drilling or permanent modifications.

**Q: What size sliding glass dog door does a large dog need?**
A: Start with the weight rating and the flap. Both PetSafe Sliding Glass Pet Door Large panels (the standard-height and the 96-inch) are rated for pets up to 100 lb; the Extreme Weather panel's rating is not among the verified facts. The standard-height one has a 10 1/4 x 16 3/8 inch flap. Both Ideal Pet panels are rated for pets up to 90 pounds with a 10-1/2 x 15 inch flap. The AKC warns that a door that is too small could be frightening or even dangerous if a dog gets stuck.

**Q: My sliding door is 96 inches tall. Which panel fits?**
A: Among these picks, only the PetSafe tall panel is listed for that height. PetSafe Support says "The 96 inch sliding glass pet door is adjustable from 91 ⁷⁄₁₆ to 96 inches." The PetSafe standard panels and the Ideal Pet 10-1/2 x 15 panel top out at 80 11/16 inches or lower; check the Modular's height on its listing.

**Q: Will a patio panel work on a vinyl sliding door?**
A: Not the Ideal Pet panels: their listings are for aluminum sliding patio doors. The verified facts for the PetSafe panels name no frame-material restriction, but the tall panel's listing does require a track at least 1 inch deep. Check your track before ordering.

**Q: Is a patio panel dog door secure?**
A: Only as secure as the way you close it. AKC Reunite warns that other people's pets, wildlife and even people enter homes through pet doors. Choose a panel with a closing panel, security bar or lock-out slide, and install it only where it opens into a securely enclosed yard or run, as the AKC advises.
