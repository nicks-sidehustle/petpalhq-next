Cats spend a lot of the day asleep, but not in one long stretch. The Ohio State Indoor Pet Initiative says cats don't sleep in one block: "Rather, they sleep and wake frequently throughout the day and night." Cats Protection, the UK cat charity, says the same and adds where they like to do it: "Cats rest or sleep intermittently throughout the day and often prefer to sleep in a warm, comfortable, elevated and safe place." A bed gets used over and over, so it is worth choosing one your cat will feel safe in and you can keep clean.

This guide ranks three non-heated beds for indoor cats: a covered cat cave, a cat house with an enclosed den and a top platform, and an open calming donut. Heated and self-warming beds, window perches and cat trees are left out. Three picks is what the data supported. Other candidates were dropped because their Amazon listing is written for dogs only, they could not be verified, or they are heated.

These picks are editorial synthesis. PetPalHQ does not run a product-testing lab, and no bed here was tested with a cat. Each was chosen by reading its Amazon listing and, where one was available, the maker's product page, against guidance from Ohio State, the ASPCA, Cornell Feline Health Center, Cats Protection and PDSA. Each card's price is for the one size and color it names. Confirm current price and availability before buying.

## Why Covered Beds Rank First

The ranking leans on one point the sources agree on: cats rest where they feel safe. Ohio State says cats are at their most vulnerable while sleeping, so they choose resting spots that feel safe and secure. For indoor cats it is more specific: "This is why it is especially important to provide indoor cats with hiding areas; these spots must permit the cat to feel safe from people, loud noises and other animals". A covered bed does double duty as a hiding place, which is why the Meow Hut and the Bedsure Cat House score highest on enclosure. A donut with raised bolsters gives a cat walls to lean on but no cover.

That does not make an open bed wrong. Some cats sleep stretched out in the open, and a cat that ignores a cave may take to a donut. If you are unsure, the [cat pheromone diffusers guide](/guides/best-cat-pheromone-diffusers-calming-2026) covers a calming angle for anxious cats, and the [cat trees guide](/guides/best-cat-trees-large-cats-2026) covers the elevated perches Cats Protection says cats often prefer.

## Washing, Drafts and Older Cats

The ASPCA's advice is short: "Your pet should have her own clean, dry place in your home to sleep and rest." It adds: "Be sure to wash the bedding often." That is why washability carries a quarter of the score. The Meow Hut and the Small Calming Donut can go in the washer whole, per their maker's pages, while the Bedsure Cat House lists only its cushion covers as machine washable.

Placement matters more as a cat ages. Cornell Feline Health Center says: "Make sure your older cat's favorite soft bed or resting place is not in a drafty area of your home." Cornell also notes that a cat with joint problems "may seem lethargic and may increasingly seek out comforting places to nap", and PDSA advises offering a cat with arthritis a selection of comfortable beds it can reach easily. A floor-level bed like the donut or the Meow Hut is easier to reach than the Bedsure's top platform. For ramps and easier access around the home, see the [senior cat accessibility setup guide](/guides/senior-cat-accessibility-setup).

## No Heat in This Guide

All three beds are non-heated, and nothing here plugs in. Cats Protection notes cats like warm places, and Cornell Feline Health Center adds a caution for older cats: "Too much heat, though, can potentially burn a cat who can't move quickly, so be sure to think warm, not hot." Heated and self-warming beds are a separate category with their own safety questions. For a cat that lives partly outdoors, the [heated outdoor cat houses guide](/guides/best-heated-outdoor-cat-houses-2026) covers that setup.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Should I get a covered cat bed or an open one?**
A: Start with a covered one if you don't know your cat's preference. Ohio State says cats prefer to rest where they feel safe and secure and that indoor cats need hiding areas, which a cave or a cat house provides. If your cat already sleeps curled up on open furniture, the open Calming Donut may suit it better.

**Q: How often should I wash a cat bed?**
A: The ASPCA says to wash a cat's bedding often, without giving a schedule. The Meow Hut and the Small Calming Donut can be machine washed whole, per their maker's pages; the Bedsure Cat House lists machine-washable cushion covers. Outward Hound says not to leave the donut to air dry, because the faux fur can tangle and mat.

**Q: Is the Calming Donut a dog bed or a cat bed?**
A: Both, per its seller. Amazon titles this listing a dog bed, but its bullets say the Small size, with a 17-inch sleeping surface, is roomy enough for cats and small dogs up to 25 lbs, and Outward Hound's product page names it a cat and dog bed.

**Q: What bed is best for an arthritic cat?**
A: PDSA says "Orthopaedic memory foam beds are particularly good for cats with arthritis." None of the three picks here is a memory foam bed, and no cat-suitable orthopedic bed verified for this guide. Ask your veterinarian, and choose a bed your cat can reach without jumping.

**Q: What size cat bed do I need?**
A: Go by the weight rating on the listing. The Meow Hut card is rated for cats up to 15 lbs, the Bedsure Cat House for cats under 20 lbs, and the Small Calming Donut for cats and small dogs up to 25 lbs.
