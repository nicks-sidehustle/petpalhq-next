A house rabbit needs two spaces, not one. House Rabbit Society puts the difference plainly: "The cage is not a rabbit’s living space but rather more like a bedroom for resting." The living space is a pen, a rabbit-proofed room, or both. This guide ranks indoor rabbit housing with that split in mind: four cages to serve as the bedroom and two pens to serve as the house.

The RSPCA takes the same view of indoor rabbits: "For rabbits kept indoors, their enclosure could be an indoor pen or even a ‘rabbit proofed’ room in your house – as long as hazards are removed and your rabbit's needs can be met, this can become a safe, enriching and happy home for them." So read every cage below as one piece of a setup, not the whole of it.

## How Big Should an Indoor Rabbit Cage Be?

Measure the rabbit, not the cage first. House Rabbit Society's rule is "Any provided cage should be 3-4x the stretch out length of the rabbit." Lay the rabbit out at full stretch, or watch it flop, and multiply. A rabbit that stretches 18 inches needs a cage 54 to 72 inches long. Of the cages here, only the 60-inch cage, at 59.06 x 36.22 inches, meets the 3x rule for that rabbit. The Living World habitat is 46.9 inches long and the Wabbitat 39.5 inches, which at 3x suit rabbits up to about 15.6 and 13.2 inches. The clear-door tray cage's title gives 140 * 45 * 52 with no unit, so read its size on the listing before you order.

For the living space, House Rabbit Society names a 4 x 4 foot exercise pen with free-roam time as an ideal setup for most rabbits. Both pens here, at 47.5 x 23.7 and 48.4 x 24.8 inches, cover about half that area. The RWAF sets a higher bar for a pair: "Our recommended living area for 2 average sized rabbits (and they should be kept at least in pairs) is a single enclosed area of at least 3m x 2m by 1m high." That is roughly 10 by 6.5 feet and about 39 inches high, which is room-sized. No single product here reaches it, so for two rabbits plan on a rabbit-proofed room or several pens joined together.

## Floors and Head Room

The RSPCA wants floors solid, not wired or barred, and non-slip. Two cages here state a solid base: the Wabbitat's 5.5-inch deep base and the Living World habitat's plastic bottom base. The clear-door tray cage states a thickened plastic base over pull-out trays, so confirm the surface the rabbit stands on. Both pens include a 24 x 48 inch floor mat (PAWING's listing calls its mat waterproof). The 60-inch cage lists a removable grid tread plate over its tray; cover it or take it out. If any base or mat feels slick, add bedding or a rug for grip.

Head room is the other check. RSPCA Australia sets the minimum cage height for rabbits over 12 weeks at 45 cm, about 17.7 inches, and says the rabbit should be able to sit upright with its ears fully erect. The PAWING playpen's stated 16.1-inch height falls below that with its roof on. On a multi-level cage, check each level, not the overall height.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can a rabbit live in a cage full time?**
A: Not according to any source this guide uses. House Rabbit Society calls the cage a bedroom for resting, and the RWAF says a single enclosed area for two rabbits should be at least 3 m x 2 m by 1 m high. A cage works as a home base inside a larger pen or rabbit-proofed room, with free-roam time on top.

**Q: Is a dog exercise pen OK for a rabbit?**
A: House Rabbit Society's own example of an ideal setup is a 4 x 4 foot puppy exercise pen with access to free-roam time, so a pen sold for dogs can work if its mesh and height suit the rabbit. The two pens picked here are sold as small animal playpens, and both list about 0.5-inch mesh or grid spacing, which is the kind of detail to check on any pen.

**Q: Do I need a pen with a roof?**
A: Not always, but a roof settles two problems: a rabbit that jumps, and other pets in the same room. Both pens here list 8 transparent top panels. Check the height under the roof: the VISCOO pen's walls are 31.5 inches, while the PAWING playpen's stated height is 16.1 inches, below RSPCA Australia's 45 cm cage minimum.

**Q: Are wire-bottom cages bad for rabbits?**
A: The RSPCA says housing floors should be solid, not wired or barred, because wire can hurt rabbits' feet. If a cage has a wire floor grid over the tray, like the 60-inch cage's grid tread plate, cover it or leave it out, and give the rabbit a solid, non-slip surface to stand on.

**Q: How tall should a rabbit pen be?**
A: RSPCA Australia's 45 cm minimum applies to cages, and the RWAF's living-area standard runs to 1 m high. The VISCOO 32-panel pen's listing gives 31.5 inches, which clears the cage minimum but falls short of the RWAF figure. A roofed pen needs enough height under the roof for the rabbit to sit up with its ears raised.
