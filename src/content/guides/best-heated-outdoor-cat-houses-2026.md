A cat that lives outside through winter, whether a stray, a feral, a community cat or a barn cat, needs three things from a shelter: a source of warmth that is safe to leave running unattended, more than one way out, and a floor that is not sitting on cold, wet ground. This guide compares outdoor cat houses on those three points, and several listings that look complete leave one of them out. This guide ranks six picks on how clearly each listing covers them: two heated K&H houses, a heated house with a thermostatic pad and a raised floor, an elevated house on legs, an unheated insulated shelter for sites with no outlet, and a heated pad for a shelter you already own.

The first question is not which house to buy but whether the site has power. HumanePro notes that heated pet beds rated for outdoor use work if you have an accessible and safe electrical outlet. If you have one, the heated houses at the top of this list are the choice. If you do not, skip straight to the unheated shelter, because a heated house without power gives no heat.

These picks are editorial synthesis. PetPalHQ does not run a shelter-testing lab, and no house here has been tested outdoors by us. Each was chosen by reading its Amazon listing against shelter guidance from Alley Cat Allies, ASPCApro and HumanePro. Where a listing leaves out a figure, such as a heater's wattage or a house's size, this guide records it as missing rather than estimating it.

## Heated or Unheated: Start With the Outlet

Five of the six picks need power: four heated houses and one heated pad. Before choosing between them, confirm that there is a safe, accessible outdoor outlet within reach of the spot the shelter will sit. According to their listings, the K&H Outdoor Heated Kitty House runs on 110/120 volt current and the K&H pad on 120V only.

Where there is no outlet, warmth comes from insulation, dry bedding and the cats themselves. HumanePro's no-power option is a self-warming pad, which absorbs and holds heat without electricity but has to be kept dry. The Texsens shelter is this guide's unheated pick because its listing names its insulation, honeycomb panels and aluminum foil, and puts it on an elevated base.

## Two Exits, and a Flap on Each

A shelter with one door can trap a cat when something else comes in. That is why the escape door shows up so often in searches for outdoor cat houses, and why this guide weights exits second only to the heat source. ASPCApro's advice for a two-door shelter is short: "Add flaps to both entrances to reduce heat loss." Both K&H houses list a removable clear flap on each of their two exits. The Lslpin describes a double-door design but includes one transparent door, and the MEWTOGO lists two PVC doors. The Texsens listing names two kinds of door curtains but no second exit.

## Off the Ground

Alley Cat Allies and ASPCApro agree that a winter shelter should sit off the ground, and ASPCApro adds that raising it makes it easier for cats to warm the space with their body heat. Only three of the six picks describe elevation: the MEWTOGO stands on 6 legs, the Lslpin floor sits 3 inches up, and the Texsens has an elevated base. Neither K&H house describes a raised floor, so set either one on a pallet or a stand. The catio setup guide covers a shelter placed inside an enclosure; see [how to set up a catio](/guides/how-to-set-up-a-catio-outdoor-cat-enclosure-2026).

## Size It to the Cats, Not Bigger

It is tempting to buy the largest house for a colony, but Alley Cat Allies says "Larger isn’t always better, because the heat will disperse quickly, and the cats will need a warm shelter during the winter." It also gives a target for a group shelter: "A space large enough for three to five cats to huddle is perfect." For two cats, the K&H Extra-Wide is sized for the pair, and the MEWTOGO's listing title says 1-2 cats. No house on this page lists a capacity above two cats. If you are also sheltering a dog outside, the [insulated and heated dog houses guide](/guides/best-insulated-heated-dog-houses-2026) covers dog-sized houses and furnaces, and the [catio enclosures roundup](/guides/best-catio-outdoor-cat-enclosures-2026) covers enclosures for owned cats.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Are heated outdoor cat houses safe to leave plugged in all winter?**
A: That depends on what the listing documents, which is why this guide weights the heat source highest. The K&H Outdoor Heated Kitty House lists a MET Safety Listed heated bed and says the whole product is certified by MET Labs, and the Extra-Wide's listing describes its heated bed as safety listed. The Lslpin lists UL certified heating wires and a V-0 flame-retardant pad. The MEWTOGO house publishes no wattage or safety listing for its heater. Whatever you buy, it only belongs where there is an accessible and safe outdoor outlet, as HumanePro puts it.

**Q: Why doesn't the heated bed feel warm when I touch it?**
A: The K&H Outdoor Heated Kitty House listing explains this directly. Its heated bed gives off a small amount of heat at rest and only warms to its maximum when a cat is using it, so a cool bed in an empty house is how it is designed to work. The K&H Extreme Weather Kitty Pad works the same way, warming when a cat is using it.

**Q: What can I use for a feral cat shelter with no electricity?**
A: An insulated, raised, unheated shelter such as the Texsens, with dry bedding inside. HumanePro notes that self-warming pet pads absorb and hold heat without electricity but need to be kept dry. Dryness is the recurring theme: a raised floor keeps ground damp out, and washable mats make it easier to swap wet bedding for dry.

**Q: Can I put a blanket over the heated pad to make it cozier?**
A: Not on the K&H Extreme Weather Kitty Pad. Its listing says not to cover the pad with blankets, towels, bedding or other materials, and to use only the K&H cover made for it, which is sold separately. Check the instructions for any other heated pad before layering anything on top of it.

**Q: How many cats can share one outdoor cat house?**
A: Two picks state a capacity: the K&H Extra-Wide's listing says it is large enough to accommodate 2 cats, and the MEWTOGO's listing title says 1-2 cats. Alley Cat Allies says: "A space large enough for three to five cats to huddle is perfect." It also warns: "Larger isn’t always better, because the heat will disperse quickly, and the cats will need a warm shelter during the winter." No house on this page lists a capacity of three or more cats.
