## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is the more expensive GPS fence always the better long-term value?**
A: Not necessarily, and this category is a clean counterexample. The SpotOn Nova costs $999.00 upfront with no required subscription, so its 3-year total cost is also $999.00. The Halo Collar 5 costs less upfront at $524.00, but a subscription is mandatory to activate its GPS and fence features, and Halo's own current plans run $8.49 to $16.99 a month — pushing Halo's real 3-year cost to $829.64-$1,135.64, which can exceed SpotOn's flat number on the higher tiers. The cheaper sticker price does not guarantee the cheaper total cost.

**Q: What's the actual cheapest way to get real GPS containment?**
A: The PetSafe Guardian GPS Dog Fence, at $399.99 with no subscription of any kind, ever. Its 3-year total cost is the same as its price on day one, and it's less than half of even Halo's cheapest available plan tier over the same period. The trade is losing live tracking and portability, which is a real limitation for some households, but for pure fixed-yard containment on a budget, nothing in this comparison beats that number.

**Q: Should I just buy a GPS tracker instead of a fence to save money?**
A: Only if the job is different from containment. A tracker like Tractive is genuinely the cheapest device in this comparison — about $357.97 over three years — but it does not contain a dog. It reports location and alerts you to a boundary crossing; it does nothing to stop the dog from crossing it. A tracker paired with a physical fence is a smart, cheap addition; a tracker bought instead of any fence, expecting it to keep a dog in the yard, will not do that job regardless of price.

**Q: Why is the Garmin system included if it's the most expensive option here?**
A: To show the flat-cost pattern holds outside the backyard-fence category too, and to draw an honest line around who it's actually for. The Garmin Alpha 300i tracks and trains via a direct radio link to the collar rather than cellular GPS, so it needs no subscription for that core function — its 3-year cost is its $1,223.98 sticker price, identical in structure to SpotOn's flat-cost model. But it's built and priced for hunting and working-dog use at real range, not as a premium backyard-containment upgrade, and it's the wrong comparison for a typical yard-containment need.

**Q: Does the built-in Garmin inReach feature cost extra?**
A: Only if you want to use it, and only for a feature unrelated to the dog. inReach's two-way satellite messaging, SOS, and weather features require a separate, active Garmin inReach subscription — but that's a human-safety feature for remote areas with no cell service, not a dog-tracking or training function. Skipping the inReach plan changes nothing about how the Alpha 300i tracks or trains a dog.

**Q: How often do these subscription prices change?**
A: Regularly enough that this guide states its numbers as of a specific verification date rather than as permanent facts. Halo, SpotOn, and Tractive all run promotional pricing, tier changes, and regional variation on their plans pages, so confirm current pricing directly on each brand's site before buying, especially if you're deciding based on a specific multi-year total from this guide.
