Winter is hard on paws in ways that have little to do with the cold itself. Salt and other deicers dry out pads and irritate the skin between the toes, snow packs into balls between the pads, and whatever a dog walks through gets licked off later. VCA Animal Hospitals sums up why boots help: "Booties not only protect your dog from chemical irritation resulting from salt-treated sidewalks or streets, but they also prevent snow and ice balls from forming between those delicate little toes!"

This guide ranks four boots on four points: how well they protect against snow, cold and chemicals, what is under the paw, how well they stay on, and how clearly the maker explains sizing. The picks are Ruffwear's redesigned Polar Trex winter boot, the fleece-lined Muttluks Snow Mushers, Ruffwear's all-terrain Grip Trex and a 12-boot pack of PawZ natural-rubber boots. Hot-pavement-only boots and dog coats are left out; for coats, see the [premium dog winter coats guide](/guides/best-premium-dog-winter-coats-2026).

These picks are editorial synthesis. PetPalHQ does not run a gear-testing lab, and no boot here was worn for this guide. Each was chosen by reading the maker's product page against VCA, ASPCA, AKC and PetMD guidance, and every waterproofing, lining and traction description on this page is the maker's own. Each card's price is for the one pack and size it names, and price varies by size. Confirm current price and availability before buying.

## Does My Dog Need Boots?

The American Kennel Club offers a simple test: "As a general rule of thumb, if you wouldn’t walk barefoot on a surface, such as a scalding or freezing sidewalk, neither should your dog." Salted sidewalks are the main reason to reach for boots. The ASPCA says "Booties can provide even more coverage and can also prevent sand and salt from getting lodged between bare toes and causing irritation." Snow is the other. The AKC says of the snowballs that form between the pads: "Snowballs are uncomfortable for your dog, and put them at risk of harming themselves."

A dog not used to the cold needs time as well as gear. VCA says: "However, if your dog isn’t used to the cold, you may want to limit walks to 15 to 20 minutes at first before working up to 30 minutes."

## Why Salt and Ice Melt Matter

Deicers are a paw problem and a swallowing problem. VCA says: "Not only are rock salt and other salt-based deicers toxic if eaten directly off the ground or licked off paws once inside, but these deicers are also very drying to your dog’s paw pads and delicate skin between their toes." It adds: "Sometimes deicers even include ethylene glycol (antifreeze), which is poisonous to pets." The ASPCA calls antifreeze lethal for dogs and cats. A boot keeps that off the paw on the walk, which also keeps it out of the dog's mouth when it licks its feet at home.

## How to Measure for Boots

VCA's advice is short: "Be sure to get properly fitting boots by using the manufacturer’s sizing guidelines." The makers here measure differently. Ruffwear sizes both the Polar Trex and the Grip Trex by paw width: "Measure across widest part of the paw when bearing weight. If between sizes, size down. Dogs can have larger front paws than rear paws - measure all paws." PawZ sizes by paw length. Muttluks uses its own numbered sizes on its boot sizing page, while Amazon lists the Snow Mushers in letter sizes, so check the Muttluks chart.

The AKC describes a paper method for measuring width: press the dog's paw into a sheet of paper, which the AKC says mimics the splay of the foot while walking, trace the indentation, then "Draw a line across the widest part of the paw tracing and measure it." Ruffwear's method likewise measures the paw while it bears weight.

Count the boots, too. The Ruffwear and Muttluks picks each come as 2 boots, and Ruffwear sells them that way so the front and rear pairs can be different sizes. PawZ come 12 to a pack.

## Getting a Dog Used to Boots

The AKC's first step is a small one: "Start with having your dog wear one boot." Once the dog is used to one, the AKC adds the next boot, then suggests short indoor sessions that get longer each time before the first real winter walk. VCA's fit test applies throughout: "Make sure they’re easy to get on and off, but not too easy—you don’t want your dog kicking them off as soon as you strap them on!" Ruffwear also advises checking the fit and the strap tension 15 minutes into an outing and adjusting as needed.

## If Your Dog Won't Wear Boots

Some dogs never accept boots. Paw balm is the fallback VCA describes: "The balm also creates a barrier between your pet’s paws and salt or other deicing agents that are applied to streets and sidewalks." Musher's Secret is one paw wax of this kind; it is covered as a paw-care pick in the [nail trimming and paw care guide](/guides/how-to-trim-your-dogs-nails-at-home-paw-care-2026). A balm is a barrier, not a boot, so the after-walk cleanup below still applies.

## After the Walk

Boots or not, clean the paws when you come in. The ASPCA says: "After each walk, make sure to wash and dry your pet's feet and stomach to remove ice, salt and chemicals." VCA adds: "Once you’re back home wipe down your pet’s paws with a damp towel and check those tender tootsies to remove any remaining ice balls." While you are there, look for cold injury. VCA says: "Check your pet’s paws (and their ears and tail too) for signs of frostbite (swelling, pain or tenderness, blisters, pale or blue skin)." For a dog that spends long stretches outdoors, boots are no substitute for shelter; the [insulated and heated dog houses guide](/guides/best-insulated-heated-dog-houses-2026) covers that.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do dogs need boots in the snow?**
A: Not every dog on every walk, but boots help where salt, deicers or packed snow are a problem. The AKC's rule of thumb is that if you wouldn't walk barefoot on a freezing sidewalk, neither should your dog, and VCA says boots protect against salt irritation and stop snow and ice balls forming between the toes.

**Q: How many boots come in a pack?**
A: It depends on the brand. The Ruffwear Polar Trex and Grip Trex and the Muttluks Snow Mushers each come as 2 boots, so four paws take two packs. PawZ come 12 to a pack.

**Q: Should I size up or down for dog boots?**
A: For both Ruffwear boots, Ruffwear says to measure across the widest part of the paw while the dog bears weight, measure all paws, and size down if your dog is between sizes. PawZ sizes by paw length, and Muttluks uses its own numbered chart, so follow each maker's guide, as VCA advises.

**Q: Are rubber dog boots a good idea?**
A: PetMD advises caution: "It’s best to stay away from latex or rubber boots as they can trap moisture and cause itchy paws." PawZ boots are natural latex, and PawZ itself recommends removing them after one or two hours.

**Q: What if my dog refuses to wear boots?**
A: Start with one boot, as the AKC suggests, and keep sessions short. If your dog still won't wear them, VCA describes paw balm as a barrier against salt and deicers, and the paws still need wiping down after every walk.

**Q: Are the prices on this page the same for every size?**
A: No. Each card's price was checked on Amazon on November 15, 2026 for the one pack and size it names, and price varies by size. The PawZ listing title read did not name a size, so check the price for your dog's size on each listing.
