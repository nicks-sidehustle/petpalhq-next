Not every dog needs a winter coat. The American Kennel Club says: "Generally, large breed dogs with thick coats will be protected from the cold." PDSA, the UK veterinary charity, says "Most dogs will be fine with short periods in the cold without any additional layers, but some definitely need a bit of extra warmth!" This guide is for the dogs in that second group: small and toy dogs, short-haired and hairless dogs, clipped dogs, low-slung dogs, and puppies and older dogs, walked in weather that is cold and wet at the same time.

For those dogs, a coat has to do four things. It has to keep water out, keep warmth in, go on over the harness the dog already walks in, and fit, because a coat that is too loose or too tight causes its own problems. This guide ranks seven premium coats on those four points: two Hurtta coats, three Ruffwear waterproof pieces including a full-body coverall, a packable Ruffwear puffer for dry cold, and a light reversible Kurgo layer. Raincoats, fleeces and sweaters are left out.

These picks are editorial synthesis. PetPalHQ does not run an apparel-testing lab, and no coat here was worn for this guide. Each was chosen by reading the maker's specifications against AKC, PDSA and Cornell guidance, and every waterproof, insulation and temperature figure on this page is the maker's own. Each card's price is for the one size and color it names, and other sizes may be priced differently. Confirm current price and availability before buying.

## Which Dogs Need a Coat

The sources agree on who feels the cold. The AKC says small and toy breeds can't maintain their body heat in cold weather, that hairless breeds need to wear jackets outside in the cold, and that clipped or shaved dogs, Poodles included, can become cold quickly. It adds that "Young puppies and senior dogs of all breeds and sizes are more susceptible to the cold than healthy adult dogs." Cornell's Dr. Brian Collins makes the same point about coat length: "Small and short-haired dogs may need coats for short walks in cold, windy weather."

Height matters too. The AKC says dogs that are low to the ground can struggle because their bellies get wet on snow and ice, and that even Pembroke and Cardigan Welsh Corgis, despite thick fur, can benefit from a coat because of their short stature. Those dogs are the case for full-body coverage, which is why the Powder Hound coverall is on this list.

## Waterproof, Water-Resistant or Neither

PDSA's description of a multipurpose dog coat is the standard this guide holds the picks to: "A multipurpose dog coat is waterproof, windproof and has a layer of fleece or padding to keep your dog warm." The shells here fall into three groups. The two Hurtta coats publish water-column ratings and say which seams are taped, but Hurtta puts a rain limit on both. The Extreme Warmer III's page says it is "not intended to be used as a raincoat", and the Expedition Parka II's page gives two different seam statements, calls its shell membrane water- and wind-resistant, and says the jacket is not designed for heavy rain. Ruffwear rates the Vert, the Powder Hound jacket and the Powder Hound coverall at 10,000 mm, and lists Bemis seam tape on both Powder Hound pieces; the coverall's page also says its seams are taped. The Kurgo Loft is water-resistant, and the Quinzee's shell sheds light moisture only, which makes it a dry-cold layer rather than a wet-weather coat.

## Measure Twice: Girth for Ruffwear, Back Length for Hurtta

The AKC says sizing a coat usually takes two measurements, the length of the body and the girth around the widest part of the chest. Ruffwear and Hurtta lean on different ones. Ruffwear sizes the Vert, the Quinzee, the Powder Hound jacket and the Powder Hound coverall around the widest part of the rib cage, and Hurtta says back length decides the size of its jackets. Kurgo sizes the Loft from a neck and chest chart. PDSA describes measuring length from the neck at the collar to the base of the tail.

The between-sizes advice also differs. Ruffwear says to size down on the Vert and Quinzee but up on the Powder Hound jacket, and Hurtta says to size up on both the Extreme Warmer III and the Expedition Parka II. Get it wrong in the loose direction and the AKC's warning applies: "Jackets also shouldn’t be so loose as to present a hazard where a dog could become entangled in the coat while playing or walking." Get it wrong in the other direction and PDSA's signs show up: "If they bite or scratch at the coat or rub themselves on the floor or furniture, that’s a sign it doesn’t fit right and is irritating them." PDSA also notes that longer dogs such as Greyhounds, Lurchers and Whippets need coats designed for their dimensions. A dog that pulls on walks may also need a harness under the coat; the [no-pull harness guide](/guides/best-no-pull-harnesses-leash-manners-2026) covers those.

## A Coat Is Not a Reason to Stay Out Longer

A coat buys comfort, not unlimited time outside. Quoted by the AKC, Dr. Jolee Stegemoller, DVM, says: "As a general recommendation, if the windchill is below 20 degrees, playtime should be kept short, [in] 15–20-minute sessions, and then give the dog time to warm up inside." Cornell's Dr. Lauri-Jo Gamble gives the signs to watch on a cold walk: "Keep them short and watch for signs of hypothermia such as shivering, anxiety and moving slowly." For a dog that sleeps outside, a coat is no substitute for shelter; the [insulated and heated dog houses guide](/guides/best-insulated-heated-dog-houses-2026) covers that. For winter camping, the [camping sleep gear guide](/guides/best-premium-dog-camping-sleep-gear-2026) covers the dog's bed and pad.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does my dog need a winter coat?**
A: It depends on the dog more than the weather. The AKC says large dogs with thick coats are generally protected from the cold, while small and toy breeds, hairless breeds, clipped dogs, low-slung dogs, puppies and seniors are the ones that struggle. Cornell adds a sign to watch for: "If a dog is picking up its feet or seems reluctant to walk this may be a sign that he/she is too cold."

**Q: Should I size up or down when my dog is between sizes?**
A: It depends on the coat. Ruffwear says to choose the smaller size for the Vert and Quinzee and the larger size for the Powder Hound jacket. Hurtta says to choose the larger size for both the Extreme Warmer III and the Expedition Parka II. For the Powder Hound coverall, Ruffwear's general sizing note says to size up for a more relaxed fit or down for a more snug fit. Measure first: Ruffwear sizes the Vert, Quinzee, Powder Hound jacket and Powder Hound coverall by rib-cage girth, Hurtta sizes its jackets by back length, and Kurgo sizes the Loft by neck and chest. Kurgo's page gives no between-sizes rule for the Loft.

**Q: Can my dog wear a coat over a harness?**
A: Several of these can. The Powder Hound jacket has leash portals on the chest and back; the Powder Hound coverall, the Vert and the Quinzee each have one on the back; and the Kurgo Loft's listing title names a 2-way zipper for harness access. The two Hurtta coats have a harness opening on part of their size ranges, and the Extreme Warmer III's smallest size, 25, has a built-in harness and leash loop instead, so check the size you are ordering against Hurtta's chart.

**Q: Should my dog wear the coat indoors?**
A: No. PDSA says "Most dogs won’t need to wear a coat indoors as they can quickly overheat in modern, centrally heated homes." Take the coat off when you come back in.

**Q: How do I look after a dog coat over the winter?**
A: Wash it, and dry it before putting it away. Dr. Jolee Stegemoller tells the AKC that coats need laundering because they collect hair and dander and can become breeding grounds for bacteria, especially if they are put away damp after time in the snow. Follow the maker's care label for each coat.

**Q: Are the prices on this page the same for every size?**
