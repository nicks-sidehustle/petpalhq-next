The best place to store dry dog food is the bag it came in. The FDA says: "Store pet food and treats in the original container or bag." Its next instruction is the one that shapes this guide: "If you want to store dry pet food in another storage container, put the entire bag into the container rather than pour the kibble directly into it." A storage container, in other words, is a second layer around the bag, not a replacement for it.

That changes what a good container needs. It has to hold your bag size, close well, and be easy to wash between bags. This guide ranks seven containers on those points: a stainless simplehuman can with a gasket seal, a Buddeez roll-away built to dispense from the original bag, three IRIS bins for 10, 30 and 40 lb bags, a stackable Vittles Vault and a small OXO POP container. Auto-dispensing hoppers, feeders and bowls are covered in other guides.

## Keep the Bag: What the FDA, AKC and Virginia Tech Say

The three sources agree that the original bag comes first. The FDA gives a practical reason: the bag carries the UPC code, lot number, product name and best-by date you need if a food is recalled. Virginia Tech Extension's FST-433 says to "Store pet foods and treats in the original container", for the same reason. The AKC adds a nutrition reason: "Keeping food in its original bag helps prevent nutrients from leaching out of the food into the container, so it may be better to continue to scoop their meals rather than keeping pre-portioned meals in individual containers."

The AKC also reports the strongest view. Dr. Jennifer Larsen, a professor of clinical nutrition at the UC Davis Veterinary Medical Teaching Hospital, says pet food bags are engineered to slow the fat oxidation that makes kibble go rancid, and the AKC writes: "Even though pet food packaging isn’t airtight or watertight, Dr. Larsen advises against transferring food from its original packaging into a separate storage container or zip-close bag." For extra protection, she says the whole sealed bag can go inside a secondary container.

If you do pour the kibble out, the FDA sets three conditions: "If you need to pour the dry pet food into another storage container, make sure it’s clean, dry, and has a lid that fits snuggly." The AKC puts it the same way: "Make sure the container you use is airtight, clean, and dry". The FDA also suggests taping the bag's lot number and best-by date to the outside of the container.

## Sizing a Bin to the Bag

Every capacity on this page is the seller's figure, and the sellers say so. IRIS writes that its 40 lb bin "Holds up to 40 lbs. of dry food; capacity varies by food type." Gamma2 tells Vittles Vault buyers to "Choose the right size dog food container based on your pet's kibble shape and density". One IRIS 30 lb listing sold by Spreetail goes further: it says its bin "holds approximately 22–26 lbs of most dry kibble" and that "not all 30 lb bags will fit".

The simplehuman listing gives a range instead of one number, 36-44 lb, and says it is "sized for typical 40 lb retail bags". The Buddeez listing gives only 80 quarts, or 75.71 liters, with no figure in pounds. So read each figure as a ceiling. If your dog eats large kibble, or you plan to keep the food in its bag, buy a size up or measure the bag against the bin's listed dimensions.

## Where to Keep It, and When to Wash It

The FDA says: "Store dry pet food in a cool and dry place." Virginia Tech Extension gives a number: "Store dry pet food, and unopened, canned wet pet food in a cool (below 80°F) and dry place." A garage or shed that gets hot in summer does not meet that, whatever bin you use.

Wash the bin between bags. The FDA says: "Wash and dry the storage container between finishing up one bag of kibble and filling it with another to get residual fat and crumbs off the container’s surfaces." That is why this guide scores cleaning features: the simplehuman's inner bucket lifts out, the OXO's lid comes apart and is dishwasher safe, and the 10 lb IRIS has a wide top. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Should I pour dog food into a container or keep it in the bag?**
A: Keep it in the bag. The FDA says to put the entire bag into the container rather than pour the kibble directly into it, and the AKC reports that Dr. Jennifer Larsen of UC Davis advises against transferring food out of its original packaging. If you do pour it, the FDA says the container should be clean and dry with a lid that fits snugly, and that you should keep the bag's lot number and best-by date.

**Q: What size container do I need for a 40 lb bag of dog food?**
A: Start with a bin rated for at least 40 lb, then leave room. The simplehuman X-Large listing says it is sized for typical 40 lb bags and holds 36-44 lb, the IRIS 40 lb listing says capacity varies by food type, and the Vittles Vault listing says to size up for larger kibble.

**Q: Do airtight containers keep dog food fresh?**
A: Each airtight claim on this page is the seller's own. The AKC says to use a container that is airtight, clean and dry, and Dr. Larsen, quoted by the AKC, says that for added protection the whole sealed bag can go inside a container. Wash the container between bags, as the FDA advises, so old fat and crumbs do not stay in it.

**Q: Can I keep dog food in the garage?**
A: Only if it stays cool and dry. Virginia Tech Extension says to store dry pet food below 80°F in a dry place, and the FDA says to keep it somewhere cool and dry.

**Q: Do these containers work for cat food?**
