An indoor cat's favorite spot is often the window, and feline guidelines treat that as a need, not a habit. The AAFP and ISFM Feline Environmental Needs Guidelines say "Indoor resting areas, such as a perch or secluded surface, should include a location that allows the cat visibility of the outdoors." A window perch gives a cat that spot at the glass, off the floor and in the sun.

This guide ranks six window-mounted perches on four points: how much weight the maker says each one holds, whether it relies on suction cups alone, whether it folds out of the way of blinds, and whether it has room for more than one cat. ## Why a Window Perch

The AAFP and ISFM guidelines also say "Access to an elevated area increases the cat’s vertical space and allows it to monitor its environment." Clinician's Brief makes the same point about windows: "Clients can provide visual stimulation by allowing indoor cats access to windowsills or “window seats” to watch outdoor activities." Ohio State University's Indoor Pet Initiative lists perches "that attach to the windowsill" among the options, and suggests making the view more interesting with a bird feeder in the yard or flowers that attract bees, butterflies and other insects.

The view matters as much as the perch. Clinician's Brief adds a caution: "However, carefully assess what they can see so that they are not distressed by other cats or dogs." If a neighbor's cat patrols under one window, put the perch at another.

## Suction, Legs or Sill: Choosing a Mount

Four picks here hang from suction cups on the glass alone. Suction needs smooth, clean glass of the right size. K&H's listings say to clean the window and suction cups monthly, and its two hammock listings say they are not recommended for textured, louvered, or windows with divided panes. Each suction pick lists a minimum glass size, from 17 inches wide on the Universal Mount Bed to 30 inches wide and 15 inches high on the Kitty Face, so measure the glass itself, not the window frame.

The Universal Mount Bed adds adjustable snap-in legs to its suction cups, and the Unheated Kitty Sill skips suction entirely, mounting on the sill with adhesive strips or screws. If your windows are divided into small panes, or have textured glass, the sill-mounted perch is the one to look at, provided the sill is at least 24in long and 2in deep.

## More Than One Cat

The AAFP and ISFM guidelines say "Giving cats in multi-cat households choices through provision of multiple locations for hiding, perching, toileting, and food and water access will reduce fear and convey a sense of control." Ohio State adds that "More than one perch allows them to look outside at different angles." The Double Cat Window Hammock puts two levels on one mount, and K&H says the EZ Mount Kitty Sill can accommodate multiple cats. In a home with several cats, two perches at two different windows also give each cat its own view.

## Window Safety Comes First

A perch brings a cat right up to the window, so the screen matters more, not less. In a 2013 press release, the ASPCA says "Pet owners can fully safeguard their animals by installing snug and sturdy screens in all their windows." It also warns that childproof window guards do not provide adequate protection for cats. The Animal Medical Center of New York says "If a window is open too wide or if they push on a loose screen, they can fall out." Keep the window closed or behind a snug, sturdy screen whenever the perch is in use, and check the perch's mount regularly. For safe outdoor time, a [catio](/guides/best-catio-outdoor-cat-enclosures-2026) is the enclosed option, and the [behavior and enrichment guide](/guides/cat-dog-behavior-anxiety-enrichment) covers other ways to keep an indoor cat busy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Will a window perch hold a large cat?**
A: Check the maker's weight rating for the model. K&H rates the Universal Mount Bed's suction cups to hold over 60 lbs, the EZ Mount Kitty Sill, both EZ Mount hammocks and the Kitty Face to hold up to 50 lbs, and the Unheated Kitty Sill to support up to 40 pounds. These are K&H's own figures, not independent measurements, and the Double Cat Window Hammock's rating covers both levels together.

**Q: Will the suction cups stay on the glass?**
A: K&H ties the hold to upkeep: its listings say to clean the window and suction cups monthly to keep a tight seal. Its two hammock listings also say they are not recommended for textured, louvered, or windows with divided panes. If you would rather not rely on suction, the Universal Mount Bed adds snap-in legs, and the Unheated Kitty Sill mounts on the sill with adhesive strips or screws.

**Q: Can I still close the blinds with a perch mounted?**
A: With two of these, yes. K&H says the EZ Mount Kitty Sill folds up to the window so blinds and drapery can function normally, and the EZ Mount Cat Window Hammock folds down so you can still lower blinds or shades. The other listings make no fold-away claim.

**Q: Is it safe to use a window perch with the window open?**
A: Only behind a snug, sturdy screen. The ASPCA says screens in all windows are how to fully safeguard pets, and that childproof window guards are not adequate protection for cats. The Animal Medical Center of New York warns that cats on windowsills can fall if a window is open too wide or they push on a loose screen.

**Q: Which perch suits an older cat?**
A: K&H says the Unheated Kitty Sill's orthopedic foam base provides comfort for cats with arthritis or other joint issues. That is K&H's claim, not a veterinary finding. Ask your veterinarian about a cat with joint problems before adding any perch it has to jump to.
