A buried-wire in-ground dog fence is the older, more permanent answer to a question a GPS wireless fence also tries to solve: how do you contain a dog without building a physical fence. The idea is simple. A loop of wire goes a few inches underground. A transmitter sits in the garage. A collar delivers a tone, then a vibration, then — if the dog keeps going — a static correction. What that buys you over a GPS system is precision that doesn't depend on a satellite fix. The boundary sits exactly where you buried the wire, immune to the tree cover, terrain, and signal bounce that make GPS fences unreliable on rural and wooded properties. What it costs you is a weekend of trenching instead of an app setup, and a boundary you re-dig, not redraw, if it ever needs to change.

This guide covers four buried-wire systems, checked against real Amazon pricing and manufacturer specs. The range runs from a budget suburban-lot kit to a large-acreage rural setup. Every pick here uses the same static-correction method a GPS wireless fence does. So the training commitment, and the welfare questions AVSAB raises about aversive methods, apply just as much to buried wire as to a satellite boundary. The real difference is how the boundary itself gets set and maintained.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How deep does buried-wire fence wire need to be installed?**
A: Most kits in this category call for burying the wire a few inches underground — deep enough to stay below mower blades and normal foot traffic, shallow enough for a weekend DIY job with no heavy equipment. Exact depth varies by kit, so check the manual that ships with your specific system before you dig.

**Q: Does a buried-wire fence really have no subscription cost, ever?**
A: Correct. None of the four systems in this guide charge a recurring fee. That's built into the technology: the boundary is a wire and a transmitter you own, not a service the maker has to keep running. The trade is upfront labor instead of an ongoing bill — the opposite of what a subscription-based GPS fence asks for.

**Q: Can one buried-wire system contain multiple dogs?**
A: Yes, with the right kit. SportDOG's In-Ground Fence System ships with two collars and supports unlimited extra dogs with add-on collars. PetSafe's Stubborn Dog kit works with every PetSafe in-ground collar except YardMax, so a household can mix collar styles on one wire loop. The Extreme Dog Fence kit reviewed here is sold as a 1-dog kit — check that brand's multi-dog options if you need more than one collar from them.

**Q: What happens if the buried wire gets cut by a mower or edger?**
A: Most transmitters spot a break in the wire loop and stop delivering correction system-wide, rather than failing silently. Makers frame that as a safety feature: an uncorrected boundary beats a boundary that corrects a dog somewhere it shouldn't. Repair usually means finding the break and using an included splice kit to reconnect the wire — Extreme Dog Fence ships splice kits for exactly this reason. Treat this as ongoing upkeep, not a one-time install cost, especially near a fence line that gets mowed often.

**Q: Is a buried-wire fence better than GPS for a rural or wooded property?**
A: Usually, yes — that's the core split this guide is built around. GPS accuracy needs a clear view of the sky. Dense tree cover and hilly terrain are exactly the conditions that weaken a satellite fix, which is why our [Best GPS Wireless Dog Fences](/guides/best-gps-wireless-dog-fences-2026) guide flags a three-quarter-acre floor and open-sky assumptions for that category. A buried-wire boundary doesn't depend on satellite visibility at all — the wire sets the line no matter what's overhead.

**Q: How long does training take before a dog can be left unsupervised inside a buried-wire boundary?**
A: Every maker in this category documents a multi-week, flag-based training window, not a same-day setup. AVSAB's position statements on humane training explain why that window matters: a rushed introduction risks a mis-timed correction that teaches fear of the yard instead of respect for the boundary. Follow the training guide that ships with your kit, and don't skip stages to save time.

**Q: Do buried-wire fences work for small dogs?**
A: It depends on the kit. PetSafe's YardMax collar is rated for dogs as light as 5 pounds, the lowest floor in this guide. SportDOG's In-Ground Fence System is rated for dogs 10 pounds and up. PetSafe's Stubborn Dog kit fits dogs 8 pounds and up. Check the specific collar's weight rating before buying — a collar built for a larger breed can deliver a correction too strong for a small dog.
